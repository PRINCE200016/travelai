TrainsUpdated November 2026

Booking Indian Railways tickets without the stress

Book early. Reservations open 60 days before the date of travel (not counting the day of the journey). For overnight trains on popular routes and anything around a festival, book on the day bookings open.

Know what the status means. CNF is confirmed. RAC gets you on the train with a shared berth, and is usually upgraded to a full berth as people cancel. WL is waitlisted: if it's still waitlisted when the chart is prepared, a ticket booked online is cancelled automatically and refunded, and you can't board on it.

Tatkal is the late option. Tatkal booking opens one day before the journey, at 10:00 for AC classes and 11:00 for sleeper, at a higher fare. Seats go within minutes, so log in early with your passenger details saved.

Pick the class that fits. Sleeper is cheapest but can be crowded and isn't air-conditioned. 3AC is the best value for most overnight trips. 2AC has curtains and fewer berths per bay.

Check the station. Many cities have several, such as New Delhi, Hazrat Nizamuddin and Anand Vihar in Delhi. Make sure your cab is going to the right one.

Book directly on the IRCTC website or app, or any authorised agent.

HealthUpdated November 2026

Staying well at high altitude

Above about 2,500 metres, the air holds noticeably less oxygen and many people feel it. Leh is at about 3,500 metres, and passes in Ladakh and Spiti go well above 5,000. Altitude sickness doesn't care how fit you are.

  • Rest when you arrive. If you fly into Leh, spend the first 24 to 48 hours doing very little. Driving up gradually from Manali or Srinagar helps your body adjust.
  • Go up slowly. Once above 3,000 metres, try not to sleep more than 300 to 500 metres higher each night.
  • Drink water, skip alcohol for the first few days.
  • Know the signs: headache, nausea, poor sleep and breathlessness at rest. Mild symptoms often ease with rest. If they get worse, go down, even a few hundred metres, and see a doctor.

If you have a heart or lung condition, or are pregnant, talk to your doctor before you plan a trip to these areas. Ask them too about preventive medicine. This note is general information, not medical advice.
