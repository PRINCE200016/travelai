Guides/Budget travel

Travelling India on a budget

You don't need to cut the good parts to spend less. Most of the savings come from when you go, how you get there, and knowing which costs creep up on you.

Updated November 2026 · About 8 minutes to read

Budget for the whole trip, not the hotel

A cheap room an hour outside town can cost more once you add the taxis. A cheap flight with an early start can cost a hotel night. Before you compare anything, add up the five things every trip has: getting there and back, somewhere to sleep, food, getting around, and entry fees or activities. Then add about 10% for what you didn't plan for.

When you go matters most

The same room can cost twice as much in peak week. A few rules of thumb:

  • Travel in the shoulder season. The weeks just before and after the peak, such as early October in Rajasthan or September in Munnar, usually have good weather and noticeably lower rates.
  • Avoid the big weeks if you can: Diwali, Christmas to New Year, long weekends, and the May–June school holidays in the hills.
  • Go mid-week. Hill stations near big cities, like Shimla, Nainital and Lonavala, are far cheaper from Monday to Thursday.

Getting there

Trains

Trains are usually the best value for distances up to about 1,000 km. Reservations open 60 days before the journey, and popular overnight trains fill quickly, so book as soon as you know your dates. Sleeper class is the cheapest berth; 3AC costs roughly two and a half times as much and is cleaner and quieter. An overnight train also saves you a night's accommodation.

If you're booking late, Tatkal tickets open at 10:00 for AC classes and 11:00 for sleeper, one day before the journey, at a higher fare. Our short guide to booking trains has more.

Buses

State transport buses such as HRTC in Himachal, KSRTC in Karnataka and Kerala, and RSRTC in Rajasthan are cheaper than private operators and generally reliable. Overnight Volvo buses into the hills are popular for the same reason as overnight trains.

Flights

For long distances on a short trip, a flight can be worth it. Booking three to six weeks ahead usually gets a reasonable fare. Compare the full door-to-door time and cost, including airport transfers and baggage, not just the ticket.

Where to stay

  • Homestays and guesthouses are often cheaper than hotels, and the hosts are the best source of local advice.
  • Hostels now exist in most popular towns and many offer private rooms, not just dorms.
  • State tourism hotels, such as those run by HPTDC or KTDC, often have the best locations at fair prices, even if the rooms are plain.
  • Check what's included. Make sure the price you're comparing includes taxes and, where it matters, breakfast.

Eating well for less

Eat where local people eat. A vegetarian thali at a busy local restaurant is filling, freshly cooked and costs a fraction of a hotel meal. Breakfast at a street stall that's crowded at 8 a.m. is usually a safe bet. Drink bottled or filtered water, and carry a refillable bottle; many hotels and cafés will fill it.

Getting around

Use the metro where there is one: Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Kolkata, Chennai, Hyderabad, Kochi and Jaipur all have systems. App-based cabs and autos are usually cheaper than hailing one, and the fare is agreed up front. In many hill areas, taxi unions set fixed rates, so ask for the rate card rather than negotiating from scratch.

The costs people forget

  • Taxis between the station or airport and your hotel, especially late at night.
  • Entry, green or eco taxes at some hill stations and parks, plus parking and tolls if you drive.
  • Camera fees at some monuments and museums.
  • Extra baggage on low-cost flights.
  • Tips for drivers and guides.
  • Higher prices for anything bought at the top of a viewpoint.
