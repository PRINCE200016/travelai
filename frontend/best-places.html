Where to go in India, season by season

India is too big to have one best time to visit. Here's where each part of the year is kindest, how to get there, and roughly what a day costs once you arrive.

Updated November 2026 · About 10 minutes to read

The country's weather runs on three broad seasons. From October to March the plains, deserts and coasts are at their best. From March to June, as the plains heat up, the hill stations come into their own. From June to September the monsoon turns the south and west green and wet, while the high, dry Himalaya beyond the rain opens up.

About the costs: the daily figures below are approximate, per person, for a mid-range trip once you're there: a clean double room shared by two, local food, getting around and entry fees. They don't include getting there. Peak weeks, especially Diwali, Christmas to New Year and long weekends, can cost noticeably more.

October to March: forts, ghats and beaches

Cool, dry days make this the main season for most of India. It's also the busiest, so book trains and rooms for December and January early.

Agra

Go at sunrise, when the marble turns from grey to pink and the crowds are thinnest. Agra Fort and Mehtab Bagh, across the river, are worth the extra half day. The Taj Mahal is closed on Fridays.

Best months
October to March
Getting there
About 1 hour 40 minutes from Delhi on the Gatimaan Express, or 3 to 4 hours by road on the Yamuna Expressway
Roughly
₹2,500–4,500 a day

Udaipur

The most relaxed of Rajasthan's cities, built around lakes. Stay near Lal Ghat or Gangaur Ghat to walk everywhere in the old city, and take the evening boat on Lake Pichola for the view of the City Palace.

Best months
October to March
Getting there
Maharana Pratap Airport is about 22 km from the old city; overnight trains run from Delhi and Mumbai
Roughly
₹3,000–6,000 a day

Jaipur

Easy to combine with Agra and Delhi. Start at Amber Fort early, before the heat and the tour buses, and keep an evening for the bazaars around Hawa Mahal. A composite ticket covering several monuments is good value if you're seeing more than two.

Best months
October to March
Getting there
About 4 to 5 hours from Delhi by train or road; well connected by air
Roughly
₹2,500–5,000 a day

Varanasi

Take a rowing boat along the ghats at dawn, and watch the evening aarti at Dashashwamedh Ghat, held every day. The lanes of the old city are best on foot; leave the car at the edge.

Best months
October to March. Dev Deepawali, in November, is spectacular and crowded
Getting there
Lal Bahadur Shastri Airport is about 25 km out; overnight and Vande Bharat trains from Delhi
Roughly
₹2,000–4,500 a day

Hampi

The ruins of the Vijayanagara capital spread across a landscape of granite boulders and banana fields. Hire a bicycle or a moped, start early, and rest through the hottest part of the afternoon.

Best months
October to February
Getting there
Hosapete station is about 13 km away, with an overnight train from Bengaluru
Roughly
₹1,800–4,000 a day

Goa

North Goa for busy beaches and nightlife, South Goa for quieter sand and better swimming. Beach shacks generally operate from October to May. Room rates can double between Christmas and New Year.

Best months
November to February
Getting there
Two airports, Dabolim in the south and Mopa in the north; Konkan Railway trains stop at Thivim and Madgaon
Roughly
₹3,000–7,000 a day

Andaman Islands

Clear water and some of India's best snorkelling and diving, centred on Swaraj Dweep (Havelock) and Shaheed Dweep (Neil). Ferries from Port Blair sell out in peak season, so book them when you book your flights.

Best months
November to April
Getting there
Fly to Sri Vijaya Puram (Port Blair) from Chennai, Kolkata, Delhi or Bengaluru, then ferry to the islands
Roughly
₹5,000–9,000 a day

March to June: the hills

As the plains pass 40°C, hill stations fill up. Weekends in May and June are the busiest of the year, so go mid-week if you can and book early.

Shimla

The old summer capital is busy but easy to reach. The Kalka–Shimla toy train, a UNESCO World Heritage line, is slow and worth it once. For quieter stays, look at Mashobra or Naldehra just beyond town.

Best months
March to June; December and January for a chance of snow
Getting there
About 350 km from Delhi, 7 to 9 hours by road or overnight Volvo bus; or train to Kalka, then the toy train (around 5 hours)
Roughly
₹2,500–5,000 a day

Darjeeling

Tea gardens, colonial lanes and, on a clear morning from Tiger Hill, Kangchenjunga. Spring brings the first flush of tea; October and November bring the clearest views.

Best months
March to May, and October to November
Getting there
Fly to Bagdogra or take a train to New Jalpaiguri, then 3 to 4 hours by shared jeep or taxi
Roughly
₹2,500–5,000 a day

Munnar

Rolling tea estates in the Western Ghats, cool when the rest of Kerala is humid. Eravikulam National Park is home to the Nilgiri tahr, and is usually closed for a few weeks in early spring when the young are born.

Best months
September to May
Getting there
About 110 km from Kochi airport, 3½ to 4 hours by road
Roughly
₹2,500–5,000 a day

Nainital

A lake town in the Kumaon hills that works well for a long weekend from Delhi. Use it as a base for quieter places nearby, such as Bhimtal, Sattal and Mukteshwar.

Best months
March to June, and October to November
Getting there
Train from Delhi to Kathgodam, then about 35 km by road
Roughly
₹2,500–5,000 a day

Kashmir

Spring and early summer turn the meadows at Gulmarg, Pahalgam and Sonamarg green and full of flowers. A night on a Dal Lake houseboat in Srinagar is part of the trip. In winter, Gulmarg becomes India's best-known ski slope.

Best months
April to June; December to February for snow
Getting there
Fly to Srinagar; Gulmarg is about 50 km away and Pahalgam about 90 km
Roughly
₹3,500–7,000 a day

Check current travel advisories before you book.

June to September: high deserts and the monsoon

The monsoon makes much of the country wet and some of it risky: landslides close roads in Himachal and Uttarakhand most years, and swimming is off at Goa's beaches. Two kinds of trip are at their best now.

Ladakh

A high-altitude desert beyond the reach of the rain, with monasteries, salt-white lakes and some of the highest roads in the world. Leh sits at about 3,500 metres. Rest for a full two days when you arrive before going higher; altitude sickness is common and can be serious.

Best months
June to September
Getting there
Fly to Leh, or drive from Manali or Srinagar once the passes open, usually from late May or June
Roughly
₹4,000–8,000 a day, more with a private car

Check the current permit and environment-fee rules for Nubra, Pangong and other areas on the Ladakh tourism website before you go.

Spiti Valley

Like Ladakh but quieter and rougher, with villages and monasteries such as Key and Tabo clinging to bare slopes. Phone signal is patchy and ATMs are few, so carry cash.

Best months
June to September
Getting there
By road from Shimla through Kinnaur, or from Manali over Kunzum La, which usually opens in June
Roughly
₹2,500–5,000 a day

Kerala's backwaters

The monsoon is Kerala's quiet season: lush, rain-washed and cheaper, and the traditional time for Ayurvedic treatments. Houseboat rates around Alappuzha drop. If you can, time it for the Nehru Trophy snake-boat race on Punnamada Lake, usually on the second Saturday of August.

Best months
June to September for the monsoon; October to March for dry weather
Getting there
Alappuzha is about 1½ to 2 hours by road or train from Kochi
Roughly
An overnight houseboat for two with meals costs around ₹8,000–15,000, less in the monsoon
